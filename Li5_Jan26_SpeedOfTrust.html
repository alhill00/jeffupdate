Speed of Trust: 4 Cores of Credibility

Trust is not a soft concept.

It's the one variable that changes the speed and cost of everything.

Stephen M.R. Covey's Speed of Trust framework makes a simple but powerful argument: when trust is high, things move fast and cost less. When trust is low, everything slows down and gets expensive — politically, emotionally, and operationally. The good news? Trust is a skill, not a personality trait. You can build it deliberately.

The Tool

The 4 Cores of Credibility

1

Integrity

Do you do what you say? Not just when it's convenient — especially when it's not. Consistency under pressure is where integrity is proven.

2

Intent

Do people believe your motives are good? Transparency about your "why" builds this faster than results do. Hidden agendas — even small ones — erode trust faster than most leaders realize.

3

Capabilities

Are you actually good at what you do? Leaders who keep growing inspire more trust than those who coast on past success. Competence is not a destination — it's an ongoing investment.

4

Results

Do you deliver? Track record matters. Teams trust leaders who finish what they start and own it when they don't.

 

Sound Familiar?

You've been saying for two months you'll revamp the team meeting structure. It still hasn't happened. Nobody mentions it, but you can feel it — a subtle credibility gap that grows a little each week the old meeting drags on.

Small inconsistencies erode trust faster than big failures. The gap between what you say and what you do is always visible — even when nobody says anything.

Your Challenge This Week

Score yourself 1–5 on each of the 4 Cores honestly. Where are you strongest? Where's the gap? Pick one Core and make one concrete move this week to strengthen it. Trust is built in small deposits — start making one today.
 

Quick Pulse — 1 Question

Which of the 4 Cores is your biggest opportunity — and what's one thing you could do this week to strengthen it?
