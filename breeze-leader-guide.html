Who We Are
The 5 Leadership Principles
Everything we do as leaders at Breeze flows from these five principles. They're not aspirational — they're the standard.
Be Seriously Nice
Prioritize genuine care and respect while maintaining focus on team results. Kindness creates the psychological safety that enables ingenuity.
"Your team performs better when they feel seen as people, not just contributors."
Do What's Right
Demonstrate courage to lead with integrity and transparency — even when it costs you. Your decisions set the ethical standard for your entire team.
"The harder the right choice is, the more important it is to make it."
Communicate Openly
Foster honest, two-way dialogue that strengthens teamwork and aligns everyone toward common goals. Great communication prevents problems before they start.
"Tell people what you can, acknowledge what you can't, and commit to updating them."
Promote Ownership
Empower Team Members to be accountable for decisions and own their processes and deliverables. Autonomy with accountability breeds engagement and innovative solutions.
"The most valuable thing you can give a capable TM isn't approval — it's confidence in their own judgment."
Champion Others
Create an environment where Team Members can grow and develop, celebrating their efforts and impact. Their success becomes your legacy.
"A great leader is never threatened by someone outgrowing them. That's actually the whole point."

When Someone New Joins Your Team

This guide is about what YOU do as a leader when a new Team Member starts. Their success in the first 90 days is largely a function of the environment you create. Use the checklist below to make sure nothing falls through the cracks.

Before Day 1

Set them up before they walk in the door.

  • Submit IT request for equipment and system access — do this at least one week before start date. Nothing kills first-day momentum like a locked computer.
  • Confirm workspace is ready — desk, supplies, badge, parking if applicable.
  • Send a welcome message — a personal note from you before they start. It takes 5 minutes and they will remember it.
  • Block time on your calendar for their first week — a welcome 1:1, team intro, and a check-in at the end of week one.
  • Tell your team someone is joining — who they are, what they'll own, why you're excited. Set the tone before day one.
  • Prepare their first 30 days context document — current projects, key contacts, team dynamics, and anything they need to know that isn't in a handbook.
Week 1

Make them feel like they made the right choice.

  • Welcome 1:1 on day one or two — your goals, your style, how you like to work, what you expect. And listen more than you talk.
  • Introduce them to every direct Team Member — in person or by video. Don't leave this to chance or a group email.
  • Walk them through the team's work — current projects, priorities, what success looks like for the team this quarter.
  • Make sure their Workday access and NLO assignment are live — check in with onboarding if there are gaps.
  • Introduce them to 2–3 key cross-functional partners — people they'll work with regularly. Start building their network now.
  • End-of-week check-in — how is it going, what's landing well, what's confusing? Listen and adjust.
Weeks 2–4

Give them context and an early win.

  • Establish weekly 1:1 rhythm — consistent time, their agenda. Don't cancel this in the first 90 days under any circumstances.
  • Share the backstory on key projects and decisions — why things are the way they are. Context accelerates ramp-up more than any training.
  • Give them something real to own — a task with clear parameters, where they can see their impact quickly. Don't wait for month two to let them contribute.
  • Explain how decisions get made on your team — what they can decide independently, what they should loop you in on, what needs escalation.
  • Give your first piece of real feedback — something specific they did well. Use SBI. Make feedback normal from the start, not just when something goes wrong.
Month 2

Assess how they're landing and adjust.

  • 30-day check-in conversation — structured, intentional. What's going well? What's hard? What do they need from you? What surprises them?
  • Set initial performance expectations — what does good look like in their role? Be specific. Don't leave this implied.
  • Observe how they're integrating with the team — are they connecting? Any friction? Address it early before it calcifies.
  • Connect them with L&D — make sure they know what programs and resources are available to them as a Team Member.
  • Get intel from peers — informal check-in with Team Members on how the new hire is landing. Not gossip — calibration.
Month 3

Set them up for long-term success.

  • 90-day review conversation — formal, documented. What have they accomplished? Where do they stand against expectations? What's next?
  • Have a career conversation — what do they want from this role? Where do they want to grow? What energizes them?
  • Identify their first development focus — one thing to work on together in the next quarter. Connect it to a resource or program.
  • Set 6-month goals together — clear, measurable, connected to team and company priorities.
  • Confirm they feel like they belong here — ask directly. "Do you feel set up to succeed? Is there anything I should have done differently?" Their answer is data for next time.
Pro tip: The best leaders debrief their own onboarding after 90 days. What worked? What would you do differently? Use that to get better every time you hire someone new.

Payroll & Timekeeping

Approving time, pay cycles, Workday

⌄
What is my role in payroll?
You're responsible for reviewing and approving your team's timecards in Workday. Know your payroll deadlines and review time regularly — don't let it stack up at end of cycle.
What about overtime for hourly TMs?
Overtime is calculated on hours actually worked in a week — holiday pay and PTO do not count toward the 40-hour threshold. Be aware of this when scheduling around holidays.
Where do I go for payroll questions?
Start with the Payroll SharePoint page. For specific issues, email the payroll team directly.

Time Off & Leave

PTO, FMLA, Breeze Breaks, leave of absence

⌄
How does PTO accrue for non-crew TMs?
Non-crew TMs begin accruing day one but can't use PTO until day 31. Full-time: 4 hrs/pay period (Year 1), 5.54 hrs/PP (Years 2–4), 6.46 hrs/PP (Year 5+). Max balance: 216 hours. All requests submit via Workday and route to you.
What is a Breeze Break?
Unpaid time off — any hourly or salaried TM can request it voluntarily. Routes first to you, then to the first VP/Executive in the chain. Not approved until both levels approve.
A TM needs FMLA — what do I do?
Direct the TM to AbsenceResources (ComPsych): call 844-485-2840 or visit absenceresources.com. Do not manage FMLA yourself. Your role is ensuring coverage, not approving or denying the medical leave.
What is the Breeze Kindness Day?
8 hrs/year for volunteer activities (non-crew full-time). Expires Dec. 31. Not paid out at separation. Submitted via Workday.

Travel & Expenses

Booking travel, submitting expenses, reimbursements

⌄
How do I book business travel?
Book all business travel through Routespring. Always consider a Breeze flight first. COBUS partners: JetBlue, Alaska, Air Canada, JSX (positive space). Southwest for VP and above only. All COBUS via Travel Request Form.
What is the per diem?
$2.25/hour or $54/day — no receipts required. Submit post-travel.
What is NOT reimbursable?
Upgrades, TSA PreCheck, Global Entry, passports, alcohol (without CFO approval), seat selection fees, personal expenses, mileage from home to your normal work location.
Business meals — what's required?
Document names of all attendees, company/role, and business purpose. Receipt required. Alcohol requires CFO approval.

PMO & Projects

When and how to engage the Project Management Office

⌄
When should I engage the PMO?
When you have a problem with real financial weight behind it. Before you bring it, use resources at your fingertips: department spend data, budget drivers, PowerBI. You don't need a full business case — just enough to show the problem is real.
What is the PMO intake process?
1. Identify the problem — what's broken, what does it cost? 2. Size it with available data. 3. Engage PMO intake — they guide problem-statement creation. 4. Work it together — PMO brings structure; you own the outcome.
Who needs to be involved?
Every project requires an Executive Sponsor and a Project Sponsor (VP or Director). Some initiatives require SteerCo approval based on scope and investment thresholds.
Key principle: "A well-scoped problem is the fastest path to a real solution — and the best use of everyone's time."

When should I involve People Services vs. handle it myself?

What is the corrective action process?

How do I bring on a new hire?

Leadership Skills Quick Refs

Feedback, crucial conversations, effective 1:1s

⌄
SBI Feedback Model: Situation → Behavior → Impact
Situation: When and where did it happen.
Behavior: What you observed — the specific action, not a judgment.
Impact: The effect on you, the team, or the work. Then ask how they see it.
Crucial Conversations — how to prepare
Check your story — what facts do you have vs. what you're assuming? Know your goal — what outcome are you trying to reach? Find shared purpose — what do you both care about? If someone goes silent or aggressive, rebuild safety before continuing.
What should a good 1:1 cover?
Three things every time: Personal connection — how are they doing as a person? Professional connection — what's energizing or draining them? Goals — where do things stand, what do they need? Let them set the agenda. Your job is to listen, not fill time.
Most common 1:1 mistake: Canceling them. A leader who routinely cancels sends a clear message about priorities. If you must cancel, reschedule immediately and protect that time.

Communications

When and how to involve the Comms team

⌄
When do I always loop in Comms?
Reductions in force, significant policy changes affecting multiple teams, any media inquiry, statements on behalf of the company or a department, sensitive personnel situations that may become public, anything going on the company intranet or all-team distribution.
Can I communicate directly to my team without Comms?
Yes — routine team updates, project status, operational instructions, and day-to-day leadership communication are yours to own. Involve Comms when the message crosses team boundaries or could be sensitive.
When in doubt, ask first. The Comms team would rather hear from you early than clean up a message that went out wrong.

Hiring & Job Requisitions

How to submit a new position or backfill in Workday — step by step

⌄
Which path do I use?
New Position — a role that doesn't exist yet, or growing headcount (reason: "New Headcount In-Budget" or "New Headcount Out of Budget").

Backfill — replacing one person who is leaving (reason: "Recruiting > Backfill").

Multiple Backfills — replacing two or more people at once. Each gets its own req, but the submission flow is slightly different.
⚠️ Before you submit a Backfill: The departing TM's separation must already be entered and approved in Workday. You cannot submit a backfill req until Workday shows the termination date. If it's not there yet, contact People Services first.

⚠️ Do NOT assign roles on the Assign Roles screen. Leave it blank and click Next. Talent Acquisition automatically assigns the right people — adding anyone yourself creates duplicate notifications and confusion.
What are the 6 stages in Workday?
