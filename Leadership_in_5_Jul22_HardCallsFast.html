This Week

Making Hard Calls Fast

Decisions with incomplete information

You'll rarely have all the information you need.

Great leaders don't wait for certainty — they build a process for moving confidently without it. Decision paralysis doesn't always look like freezing. Sometimes it looks like "Let me look into that" — said again and again while the window closes and the team waits.
 

The Tool

The 70% Rule

70

%

If you have 70% of the information you think you need — make the call.

Waiting for 100% rarely gets you there. It usually just costs you time, trust, and momentum. Most decisions can be adjusted after the fact. Waiting cannot be undone.
 

Ask yourself the two-question test:

→  What's the cost of waiting for more information?

→  What's the cost of being wrong and adjusting after?

Most decisions are two-way doors. You can almost always walk back through. The question is whether the cost of the detour is less than the cost of standing still.
 

Then: Decide. Communicate. Adjust.

Don't just decide in your head — tell your team what the decision is and why. A communicated decision, even an imperfect one, gives people something to move toward. Silence doesn't.

Sound Familiar?

A team member is in your doorway. There's a situation developing — do you reroute, extend, escalate? You don't have the full picture. But they're looking at you. The moment is right now.

You have 70% of what you need. You've run the two-question test. The cost of waiting is higher than the cost of adjusting. Make the call. Say it out loud. Move together.

Your Challenge This Week

Catch yourself saying "let me get back to you on that" more than once this week. If you have 70% of what you need, practice making the call on the spot. Notice how it feels — and notice how your team responds.
