Leading When Everyone
Is Running on Empty

Summer ops is relentless. Your team feels it. Here's how to keep them going.
End of June  ·  Q2 Stretch Run

By the end of June, most airline teams are tired. The summer surge is real — and so is the grind. Your team doesn't need a pep talk. They need a leader who sees the weight they're carrying and gives them something concrete. Here are four ways to do that this week.

4 Energy Strategies for Right Now
1

Name the weight out loud

The most underrated leadership move: acknowledge what's real. "I know this has been a hard stretch. I see how hard you're working." You don't need to fix anything to say that. Invisible weight gets heavier when no one acknowledges it.

"This has been a grind. I want you to know I notice it."
2

Take one thing off

Energy goes up when something comes off the list. Look at your team's week and find one thing — a meeting, a report, a low-priority task — that can wait or disappear entirely. A small act of removal signals: I'm managing up so you don't have to carry it.

"We're skipping the Thursday status update. Use that hour for whatever you need."
3

Catch wins in real time — specifically

During high-pressure stretches, specific recognition hits 10× harder than usual because people are running low and feeling invisible. Don't save it for an email or a review. The moment you see it, say it. Use the formula: I noticed + what they did + why it mattered.

"I noticed how you handled that delay — you kept the whole gate calm. That's the job."
4

Protect your own tank

You can't keep refueling others from an empty tank. Your team mirrors your energy — when you're fraying, they feel it before you say anything. What does your reset look like? Name it, schedule it, and actually do it. This is not optional; it's operational.

"What's the one thing that actually restores you? Build it into this week — non-negotiably."
⚡

The myth of "push through"

The best leaders in high-pressure environments aren't the ones who grind hardest — they're the ones who know how to protect energy: their own and their team's. Stamina is a leadership skill. Model it.
