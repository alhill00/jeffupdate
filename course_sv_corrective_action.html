Foundation
If It Isn't Written Down, It Did Not Happen
Corrective action at Breeze starts and ends with one principle. You may remember every conversation you had with a Team Member. You may know exactly what was said, when it happened, and what you asked them to do differently. But if you didn't write it down, it does not exist — not in a formal process, not in a legal challenge, not in a Workday record.
This isn't a technicality. It's protection — for you, for Breeze, and for the Team Member. Documentation creates a clear, objective record of what happened, what was communicated, and what was expected. Without it, corrective action can't hold up and can't be escalated fairly.
"If it isn't written down, it did not happen."
This is the foundation of everything in this course. Start there, and the rest follows.
This course walks you through the Breeze approach to corrective action — when to act, how to document, which steps to follow, and when to involve People Services.
Foundation
When to Act — and When You're Not Ready Yet
Corrective action is appropriate when a Team Member does any of the following:
Does not meet essential job functions
New to role and not "getting it"
Is insubordinate
Violates policy or law
Has attendance issues
Misuses company resources
Causes a safety risk
Disregards our values
But there's an important distinction. The following are not enough to act on formally:
Wondering
Thinking
Feeling
Remembering
Suspecting
Rumors
Noticing
Something's annoying
Gut feelings and rumors are not documentation. You need facts — specific, observable, dateable events. If you can't write it down as a fact, you're not ready to act formally yet.

The Cost of Not Acting
Leaders who avoid corrective action aren't avoiding conflict — they're deferring it and making it more expensive. When performance or conduct issues go unaddressed, Breeze can end up in formal processes that are significantly more costly than a timely, documented conversation.
$

The real math

At an estimated $300/hour across People Services, in-house counsel, and outside counsel — a single unaddressed issue that escalates to legal proceedings can run 125+ hours. That's $37,500 before it reaches arbitration or court.

!

What that process includes

Gathering and compiling documentation, EEOC response prep and review, depositions, pre-trial preparation, arbitration — and potentially court. All of this becomes significantly harder without a documentation trail.

There's also a common mistake that sends a conflicting message: giving a pay increase at review time when there isn't merit. If a TM is under a corrective action process, a raise signals the opposite of what your documentation says.
The Three Pillars
The Three Pillars of Effective Corrective Action
Every corrective action at Breeze should be built on three pillars. Before you take any formal step, ask yourself: does this meet all three?

Consistency

In line with policies, procedures, and practices. Are you treating this Team Member the same way you'd treat anyone else in the same situation? Inconsistency is one of the most common legal vulnerabilities in corrective action.

Quantifiable

Capable of being measured, counted, or tracked. "Attitude is bad" won't hold up. "On three separate occasions in the past 30 days, the TM refused to follow a direct instruction" will.

Documentation

Objective evidence. Specific dates, specific behaviors, specific conversations. Signed and dated. Uploaded to Workday. If it isn't written down, it did not happen.

All three pillars have to be present. Consistent but undocumented won't hold. Documented but inconsistent creates legal risk. Quantified but inconsistently applied is just as problematic.
The Three Pillars
The Disciplinary Steps
Breeze uses a progressive corrective action process. These steps may be used in order — but depending on the severity of the issue, you may use some, all, or move directly to termination. Talk to People Services if you're unsure which step applies.

Training Period / Probation

Lack of fit may be identified within 1–180 days. Determined by department. Use coaching and check-ins during this window.

Coaching / Check-ins

Document your conversations. Go over specific areas for improvement with deadlines. Be positive — Breeze wants TMs to succeed. Ask: what can you do to help them get there?

Performance Improvement Plan (PIP)

Typically used for attendance and performance — when a TM isn't in trouble yet but is walking up to the line. Set specific expectations with dates. Be sure to collect comprehensive data.

Written Warning

Collect and document comprehensive data and expectations. Must be signed and dated the day it's delivered, with a witness signature. Upload to Workday.

Final Written Warning

Last chance. Provide a specific, limited timeframe to course correct. People Services must be present.

Termination

People Services must review all terminations prior to the meeting with the Team Member. Do not proceed without that review.

How to Document — and How to Quantify
Documentation doesn't have to be formal to count. The most important thing is that it happens consistently and includes the right details. Use whatever method works for you:
Email to yourself when something happens
Spreadsheet with a tab per Team Member
Notebook
OneNote
Word doc
Every entry should answer four questions: Who. What. Where. When.
Example documentation entry:
"12/4/2023 — Met with [TM Name]. Discussed declining metrics (down 24%). TM admitted to being overwhelmed. I agreed to provide additional coaching and we set a check-in for 12/18."
When quantifying, be specific. Don't write "frequently late." Write:
"On the following dates, you called in sick: March 21, March 14, February 24, February 14, January 31, January 2 and 3. Although we are only in March, you have exceeded the maximum limit per the Playbook (Section 9, a, ii)."
Always follow up in writing after a corrective conversation. A brief email confirming what was discussed and what you asked the TM to do creates an objective record — and removes any "I didn't understand" responses later.

People Services, Common Pitfalls & ADA/FMLA
When to involve People Services:
1

Before all terminations

People Services must review every termination before the meeting with the Team Member. No exceptions.

2

For all Final Written Warnings

People Services must be present for Final Written Warning conversations.

3

When ADA or FMLA may be in play

If a TM discloses or hints at a medical condition, disability, or need for leave — stop and call People Services before taking any further corrective steps. You cannot manage FMLA yourself. Direct TMs to AbsenceResources (ComPsych): 844-485-2840 or absenceresources.com.

Common pitfalls to avoid: Not documenting. Not communicating when TMs fall short. Inconsistent treatment across your team. Ignoring the issue because you're too busy. Giving a merit increase during an active corrective process. Waiting too long to communicate. Not involving People Services at the right time.

Scenario 1: The Undocumented History
One of your Team Members has been chronically late — you've observed it for months and had multiple verbal conversations about it. You've tracked it mentally and remember most of the dates. You want to issue a Written Warning. What should you do?
AIssue the Written Warning now — you know the facts even if they're not formally written down yet.
BYou cannot move forward with a Written Warning without a documentation record. Reconstruct specific dates you can confirm, start documenting from today, and contact People Services before taking the next formal step.
CHave a verbal conversation, tell them this is their last warning, and start documenting going forward.
DSkip the Written Warning since the issue is longstanding and go directly to a Final Written Warning.
Practice
Scenario 2: The Corrective Conversation
You sit down with a Team Member to deliver a Written Warning. Halfway through, they become emotional — tears, silence, then they push back and say the whole thing is unfair. The conversation stalls. What do you do?
AApologize for the difficulty of the situation, and offer to reschedule when they're feeling better.
BLet the silence sit — it's okay. When the moment passes, calmly return to the three items you planned to discuss and circle back to each of them. Don't apologize. Don't back down from the documentation.
CEnd the meeting and send the Written Warning via email instead to avoid further conflict.
DCall People Services into the room immediately to take over the conversation.
Practice
Scenario 3: ADA/FMLA Enters the Picture
A Team Member has been on a PIP for performance issues. During a check-in, they mention — for the first time — that they've been dealing with a health condition that's been affecting their ability to work. They haven't submitted any paperwork. You're mid-process. What do you do?
AContinue the PIP — they haven't submitted formal documentation so the process moves forward as planned.
BPause the corrective process and contact People Services before taking any further steps. ADA or FMLA may be in play. Direct the TM to AbsenceResources (ComPsych) for next steps.
CAsk the TM to describe their condition in detail so you can determine whether it's relevant to their performance.
DGive them two weeks off and restart the PIP when they return.

Documentation is your protection — and theirs.
Corrective action done right is fair, consistent, and documented. It protects Team Members from arbitrary decisions. It protects you and Breeze from legal exposure. And it gives every TM a clear picture of what's expected and what happens next.

Your commitments from this course:

Start a documentation log for your team today — email, OneNote, spreadsheet, or notebook. Use Who, What, Where, When.
Before any formal corrective step, ask: Is it consistent? Quantifiable? Documented? If the answer is no to any of them, you're not ready.
Contact People Services before Final Written Warnings, all terminations, and any time ADA or FMLA may be in play.
