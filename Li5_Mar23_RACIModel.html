The RACI Model

Most project failures aren't about strategy.

They're about nobody knowing who was actually in charge.

RACI is a simple accountability matrix that answers one of the most common sources of team dysfunction: ambiguous ownership. When everyone thinks someone else is handling it — or when everyone tries to handle it at once — things fall through the cracks or grind to a halt. RACI gives every task a clear owner and a clear role for everyone else involved.

The Tool

The RACI Matrix

R

Responsible

Who does the work? Only one person should own this for any given task. Multiple R's is a warning sign — it usually means nobody owns it.

A

Accountable

Who is ultimately answerable for the outcome? Often the leader. Only one person here too. The buck stops with the Accountable — not with the team.

C

Consulted

Who needs to provide input before decisions are made? Two-way communication. Their feedback shapes the work before it's finished.

I

Informed

Who needs to know what happened, after the fact? One-way communication. They don't shape the work — they just need to know the outcome.

 

Sound Familiar?

Your team launches a new Guest check-in process. Three people think they're responsible for training the staff. Two of them create conflicting materials. The ops leader finds out the day before go-live.

No RACI = three people doing one job and one job not being done at all. Clarity on ownership is the first step to execution that actually works.

Your Challenge This Week

Take one current project or initiative and map it to RACI. If you can't easily name who is Responsible, that's your answer. If everyone owns it, nobody owns it — and that's a problem worth solving this week.
 

Quick Pulse — 1 Question

Think of your most recent project. Can you name one clear Responsible owner — or does that answer feel complicated?
