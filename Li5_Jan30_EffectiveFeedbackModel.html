Effective Feedback Model

Most feedback doesn't land because it's vague.

"Good job" and "that didn't work" are reactions. Not feedback.

Effective feedback is one of the most powerful tools a leader has — and one of the most underused. Not because leaders don't care, but because they haven't been given a simple model that makes it concrete and safe to deliver. The SBI model does that in three steps.

The Tool

The SBI Feedback Model

S

Situation

Set the scene specifically. "In this morning's gate briefing..." Not "lately" or "sometimes." Vague situations invite defensiveness.

B

Behavior

Describe what you observed — not what you inferred. "When you interrupted Jordan twice before they finished their point..." Not a judgment about their intent. Just what happened.

I

Impact

Share the effect — not a verdict. "...it made it harder for the team to follow the discussion and I noticed Jordan disengage." A consequence, not a character assessment.

 

Sound Familiar?

You've been meaning to address the way one of your leaders runs team meetings — they dominate the conversation and others have stopped contributing. You've hinted at it twice. Nothing changed.

Hints aren't feedback. They're wishful thinking. SBI turns a hard conversation into a specific, respectful, actionable one.

Your Challenge This Week

Identify one piece of feedback you've been avoiding or softening. Draft it using SBI before the end of this week. You don't have to deliver it today — but write it out. Clarity on paper leads to courage in person.
 

Quick Pulse — 1 Question

Is there someone on your team who deserves more honest, specific feedback than they're currently getting from you?
