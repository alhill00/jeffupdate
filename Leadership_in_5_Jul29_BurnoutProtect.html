Protecting Your Team from Burnout

Sustainable pace in peak operations

You can't pour from an empty cup.

But you also can't ask your team to.

Summer ops is a marathon disguised as a sprint. Leaders who ignore team fatigue don't get more productivity — they get turnover, errors, and people who stop speaking up when it matters most. The good news: you don't have to fix everything. You just have to notice it first.

The Tool

3 Signals Your Team Is Running on Fumes

01

Shorter fuses.

Small things are setting off people who normally handle pressure well. Patience is thin. Tone has changed. Minor friction becomes conflict.

02

Quieter voices.

The team has stopped offering ideas or flagging problems. In meetings, people wait to be asked. The proactive energy has gone quiet.

03

Slower starts.

People who used to hit the ground running need longer to engage. Energy is low at the start of shifts. Enthusiasm feels performed, not real.

When you see these signals — name it, without drama.

 

"I've noticed we've had a hard few weeks. I want to check in — what's one thing that would make next week even slightly better?"

That conversation itself — the act of noticing out loud — restores trust faster than any solution you could offer.

Sound Familiar?

August is here. Your team has been running hard since Memorial Day. You're proud of them — genuinely. But you can see it in their eyes. The energy that was there in May just isn't there anymore.

You don't need a grand gesture. You just need one real conversation. Not about the work. About them.

Your Challenge This Week

Have one conversation with one team member that's just about them — not a task, a project, or a problem. Ask: "How are you doing — honestly?" Then listen without trying to fix anything.
 

Quick Pulse — 1 Question

How confident are you that you'd notice signs of burnout in your team before it became a real problem?
