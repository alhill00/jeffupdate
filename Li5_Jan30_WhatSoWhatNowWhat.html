What, So What, Now What

Most debriefs produce insight.

Almost none of them produce change.

The "What, So What, Now What" model is used by healthcare, military, and high-performance teams to turn experience into actual learning. Most leaders debrief the "What" endlessly — what happened, what went wrong, who did what. The ones who grow move all the way through the third question. That's where the change lives.

The Tool

What / So What / Now What

1

What?

Describe exactly what happened. Facts only — no interpretation, no blame. "The gate agent called in sick and nobody knew the backup protocol."

2

So What?

What does that mean? What's the impact, the pattern, the insight? "Our backup systems only exist on paper — nobody has actually practiced them."

3

Now What?

What will we do differently? Who owns it? By when? "We're running a backup drill at every base by end of quarter. Carlin owns the template by Friday." Commitment, not intention.

 

Sound Familiar?

Your team just had a rough week — a missed deadline, a miscommunication, and a Guest escalation. You hold a debrief, everyone vents about what went wrong, and then... the meeting ends. Three weeks later, the same issues surface again.

The debrief only works if you get to Now What. Analysis without action is just storytelling.

Your Challenge This Week

Apply What / So What / Now What to one recent experience — a win, a miss, or a close call. Walk your team through it together. The What is easy. The So What requires honesty. The Now What is where leadership actually happens.
 

Quick Pulse — 1 Question

Think of your last debrief. Did you get to Now What — with a name, an action, and a deadline?
