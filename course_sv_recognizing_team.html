The Recognition That Lived Only in Her Head
Morgan had a great team member, Drew. High performer, always went above and beyond. Morgan noticed — but never said much because she assumed Drew knew how valued they were.
Drew gave notice. On the way out, Drew said: "I never really felt like my work mattered here."
Morgan was stunned. She'd thought recognition — and never said it out loud.
Recognition only works when it's delivered. Thinking it, intending it, and meaning it does nothing. Saying it — specifically, directly, on time — is the only version that counts.
Foundation
Why Recognition Fails
Most recognition fails for one of three reasons:
1. It's too vague. "Great job, team!" tells nobody what they did or why it mattered.

2. It's too late. Mentioning something from three weeks ago has a fraction of the impact of same-day recognition.

3. It's the wrong mode. Public recognition for someone who hates being called out can backfire — making them feel uncomfortable, not motivated.
Effective recognition is specific, timely, and delivered in the way the recipient actually receives it. All three matter. Missing any one of them weakens the impact significantly.
Foundation
Recognition Is Not the Same as Praise
These are not the same thing. One lands and sticks. The other fades in minutes.
Praise
"You're amazing."
Recognition
"When you stayed late Tuesday to fix the manifest error before the morning shift, it prevented a delay for 200 passengers. That's the kind of ownership this team runs on."
Praise feels good briefly. Recognition lands and sticks — because it connects the person's action to real impact.
The Recognition Framework
Three Elements That Make Recognition Effective
The SBI model (Situation, Behavior, Impact) works for recognition too — not just feedback. Here are the three elements that make it land.
S
Specific
Name the exact behavior
Not "great work" — name what they did. What action, in what situation? The specificity is what makes it real.
T
Timely
The closer to the action, the stronger the impact
Same day is best. Same week is good. Three weeks later is better than nothing — but barely. Recognition reinforces behavior in the moment.
M
Meaningful
Delivered in a mode that works for this person
Public or private, verbal or written, team meeting or 1:1. Know which your team members prefer — and use it.

Your team member stayed late to help a colleague finish a project under pressure. A week later, you remember to mention it.
How could you have made this more effective?
ARecognize it now — better late than never is a real principle.
BIt would have landed better if recognized the next day with specific detail about what they did and why it mattered.
CBring it up in the next team meeting so everyone hears it.
DWrite it into their next performance review — that's the most official recognition.
The Recognition Framework
Public vs. Private — Know Your People
Some team members love public recognition in team meetings. Others cringe. Praising someone publicly who prefers private recognition can actually make them feel uncomfortable and less likely to repeat the behavior.
Know your team. A simple question — "How do you prefer to be recognized when you do great work?" — is one of the most useful things you can learn about each person.
Recognition that makes someone uncomfortable is counterproductive. It creates a mild aversive experience around good work — which is the opposite of what you want. Simply ask, then remember the answer.

Recognizing Someone Who Hates the Spotlight
You have a team member who consistently goes above and beyond but is introverted and gets visibly uncomfortable with public praise.
Your best recognition approach is:
ARecognize them publicly anyway — it's important for the team to hear it.
BWrite them a specific, thoughtful note or email that they can read privately.
CSkip formal recognition and just tell them informally in passing.
DMention their contribution in a team meeting without using their name.

Recognizing the Whole Team, Not Just the Loudest
You want to build a recognition habit but worry about recognizing some team members more than others.
The best approach is:
ARecognize everyone equally — one recognition per person per month.
BTrack who you've recognized recently and proactively look for things to recognize across the full team.
COnly recognize performance that clearly exceeds expectations.
DLet recognition happen naturally — forcing it makes it feel inauthentic.

Do You Recognize "Just Doing Their Job"?
A team member does something well — but it's part of their normal job responsibilities.
Do you recognize it?
ANo — recognition is for above-and-beyond; doing your job is the baseline.
BYes — recognizing consistent excellence at the baseline reinforces the standard you want.
CMention it in their performance review but not in the moment.
DOnly recognize it if other team members are struggling with the same expectation.
