Module 5 — The Paradox
Being kind doesn't mean avoiding hard truths.
It means delivering them with care.
The Seriously Nice Paradox
Avoiding a hard conversation isn't kindness — it's avoidance. It lets the problem grow, and it quietly tells your FA that you don't believe they can handle the truth.

The most caring thing you can do is be honest, specific, and early.

Module 5 — Framework
The SBI Model
S
Situation
Set the specific time and place — anchor the conversation in fact.
"On Tuesday's flight to BNA..."
B
Behavior
Name what they did — not who they are. Observable action only.
"I noticed you didn't acknowledge the passenger in 14C..."
I
Impact
Say what it cost — the team, the guest, the operation, or you.
"The impact was that she filed a complaint and felt ignored..."
Communicate Openly
Module 5 — The Coaching Question
"What do you think?"
Why This Question Changes Everything
When you tell someone what to do, they follow your lead.
When you ask what they think, they own the answer.

After SBI, pause. Ask: "What do you think about what happened?"

Then listen. The conversation becomes coaching, not correction.

Module 5 — The Other Hard Conversation
Not a performance issue. A retention one.
The Three-Part Conversation — Private Workbook Reflection
For the FA who's quietly pulling back — before they've said a word about leaving.

1. Name what you noticed: "I've noticed you seem a little different lately — just wanted to check in."

2. Ask the real question: "Are you doing okay? Are you good in this role?"

3. Stop talking. Wait for the actual answer.
