First Impressions

Leaders don't get a second chance to make a first impression.

But your team gets that chance every time a new face walks in the door.

The first 90 seconds of any new relationship sets a tone that can take months to reset. Whether it's a new Team Member's first day, a Guest's first flight, or your first week in a new role — the way you show up in that window matters more than almost anything that comes after it.

The Tool

The 3-Part First Impression Framework

1

Body Language First

Before you say a word, your posture, eye contact, and energy are already communicating. Stand up, face them, and smile first.

2

Name + Context

Use their name immediately and give them context — who you are and why you're genuinely glad they're there. Make it personal, not procedural.

3

One Real Question

Ask something that requires more than a yes/no. "What are you most excited about?" beats "How are you doing?" every time.

 

Sound Familiar?

A new Team Member starts on Monday. You're slammed, so you give a quick wave from across the room, point them to HR, and promise to catch up later. By the end of the week, they've told someone they weren't sure their leader even knew who they were.

Those first moments communicate your culture more clearly than any handbook. The impression you make in 90 seconds is the one they carry for months.

Your Challenge This Week

Identify one first impression you can do better — a new Team Member, a cross-functional partner, or even a recurring meeting where you usually walk in distracted. This week, use the 3-part framework in that moment. Show up first, and watch the room follow.
 

Quick Pulse — 1 Question

Think about the last new person who joined your team. What impression did they get from you in the first 90 seconds?
