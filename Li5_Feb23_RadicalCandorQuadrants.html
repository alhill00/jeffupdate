Radical Candor: Quadrant Self-Audit

You read the framework. Now the harder question:

Which quadrant do you actually live in?

Last week we introduced Radical Candor. This week is about honest self-diagnosis. Most leaders believe they're in the top-right — Radical Candor — but operate in Ruinous Empathy far more than they realize. The gap between our intentions and our actual behavior is usually widest when we think we're being kind.

The Tool

The 3-Step Quadrant Self-Audit

1

Map Your Last 5 Feedback Moments

For each one, ask: Did I actually say what needed to be said? Was I specific enough to be useful? Place each in a quadrant honestly.

2

Identify Your Default Under Stress

When things are busy or emotionally charged, where do you go? Most people have a default pattern. Ruinous Empathy is the most common default for people-oriented leaders. Recognizing it is step one.

3

Choose One Shift This Week

Pick one conversation where you've been avoiding the truth. Plan it using SBI (Situation, Behavior, Impact) and deliver it this week. One rep builds the muscle.

 

Sound Familiar?

You've been praising a Team Member's effort even though their output isn't meeting the bar. They're working hard and you don't want to discourage them. But six weeks in, nothing has changed — and now you're wondering about next steps.

Ruinous Empathy delayed the conversation that could have changed the outcome. The most caring thing you can do is tell someone the truth early enough that they can actually do something about it.

Your Challenge This Week

Do the 5-feedback audit this week. Be honest about which quadrant each one landed in. Then have the one conversation you've been avoiding. The leaders who get this right aren't naturally blunt — they're willing to be uncomfortable on someone else's behalf.
 

Quick Pulse — 1 Question

After mapping your last 5 feedback moments — which quadrant showed up most often?
