Repairing After Conflict

What you do in the 24 hours after is what actually matters.

Conflict doesn’t break relationships.

Silence after conflict does.

Every team has friction. That’s not failure — that’s evidence of real work and real opinions colliding. The difference between teams that grow stronger from it and teams that slowly fracture isn’t whether conflict happens. It’s what the leader does next.

Most leaders wait for it to resolve on its own. It doesn’t. It just goes underground.

The Tool

The 4-Step Repair Protocol
 

Step 1

Acknowledge what happened — without minimizing it.

Name it simply and directly. No reframes, no “both sides” framing, no “things got a little heated.” Call it what it was. The other person already has their version — your job is to show you’re not pretending it didn’t happen.
 

Step 2

Own your part — even if it’s small.

Leadership means you go first. Even if they hold 80% of the responsibility, you own your 20% clearly and without conditions. That act alone usually opens the other person up. People drop their defenses when they see you modeling accountability.
 

Step 3

Say what you need going forward.

Don’t let the repair conversation end vaguely. Name one specific thing: “What I need from our working relationship going forward is...” This makes repair productive, not just polite.
 

Step 4

Ask what they need. Then listen without defending.

“What do you need from me going forward?” Then stop talking. The instinct is to respond, explain, or counter. Don’t. The repair happens in the space you give them to be honest.

Put it together — here’s the full script:

“I want to revisit what happened between us. [Name the situation simply.] I know it created [name the impact on them]. For my part, I [own one specific thing you’d do differently]. Going forward, what I need is [your clear ask]. What do you need from me?”

Then stop. Let them respond.

Sound Familiar?

A tense exchange on the tarmac. A disagreement in a briefing that didn’t end cleanly. A moment that’s been quietly awkward since. You’ve told yourself it’s probably fine. It probably isn’t.

The longer unresolved friction sits, the more it shapes the relationship — and the harder it becomes to address. The first step is usually the only hard part.
