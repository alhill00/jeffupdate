Team Member Recognition

Your Team Members are doing great things you haven't acknowledged.

That silence is a leadership choice — even when it doesn't feel like one.

Recognition isn't a "nice to have" — it's a retention and performance tool. Team Members who feel recognized are more engaged, more productive, and far more likely to stay. The challenge isn't that leaders don't care. It's that recognition gets deprioritized when operations get busy. This week is about making it automatic.

The Tool

The 3 R's of Recognition

1

Real

Specific beats generic every time. "You handled that gate delay with exceptional calm and the Guests noticed" lands differently than "great job today." Be specific about what they did and why it mattered.

2

Rapid

The closer to the moment, the more it matters. Same-day recognition is significantly more impactful than end-of-week praise. Don't save it for the 1:1.

3

Right Audience

Public recognition for some, private for others. Know your people well enough to know which they prefer. The same words can feel like a gift or an embarrassment depending on who's in the room.

 

Sound Familiar?

Your highest performer just handled an incredibly difficult Guest situation alone — stayed late, de-escalated everything, and didn't complain once. You meant to say something. Then the week got busy. Now it's been 10 days.

Every day of silence chips away at what that recognition could have built. Recognition costs nothing and pays in everything.

Your Challenge This Week

Before the end of today, recognize one person on your team specifically — by name, for a specific behavior, in the right setting for them. Don't wait for the perfect moment. The right time to recognize great work is always right now.
 

Quick Pulse — 1 Question

Who on your team has done something worth recognizing in the last two weeks — and have you told them?
