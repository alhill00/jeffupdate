The 30-Second Check-In

Alex walks you through the one question that changes
the check-in from a formality to an actual conversation.

~3 minutes

Most check-ins are a formality. "How are you?" gets "Fine." "How's the team?" gets "Good." You asked. They answered. Nothing changed. This week, we're swapping that one phrase for something that gets a real answer — in 30 seconds or less.

The Swap
Stop Saying
"How are you?"
→
Start Saying
"What's one thing I can clear for you?"
Why It Works
1

It's specific — "one thing"

Asking for one thing removes the pressure of spilling everything. It gives permission to share one real issue, not a performance. You get a focused, honest answer instead of a shrug.

2

It's action-oriented — "I can clear"

You're not asking them how they feel. You're offering to remove an obstacle. That reframes you as a resource, not just a manager checking a box. It signals: I'm here to help, not just to ask.

3

It surfaces real blockers

The obstacles slowing your team down usually live in the gap between "I'm fine" and what they'd actually say if asked directly. This question opens that gap — in a way that's comfortable to answer.

The 30-Second Structure

0–10 sec

Stop and face them. Put down whatever you're carrying. Eye contact. The pause itself signals: you have my attention.

10–15 sec

Ask the question. "What's one thing I can clear for you right now?" Say it like you mean it.

15–30 sec

Actually listen — don't move yet. Resist filling the silence. The answer is coming. Let it arrive.

✓ Self-Check — How Would You Handle This?

What's your move?

You're walking through the ops area and spot one of your team members moving fast, head down — clearly in the weeds. You have about 30 seconds before you need to be somewhere. What do you do?
The Answer
B — Stop, ask the question, and listen

Option A is generic and keeps moving — it doesn't pause to actually connect. Option C is the phrase we're replacing: easy to answer without thinking, easy to brush off. Option D means the blocker sits for days and they feel invisible in the meantime. Option B takes 30 seconds and signals: I see you, I'm here to help, and I'm asking something specific. That's a leadership moment in the middle of a regular day.
