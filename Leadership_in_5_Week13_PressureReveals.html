What Pressure Reveals in Leaders
1

Your default communication style

When things are fine, most leaders communicate pretty well. Under pressure, you revert. Some go quiet. Some go loud. Some get precise, some get vague. Your team is watching to see which version shows up.

Ask yourself: "Do I communicate MORE or LESS when the stakes go up?"
2

Whether you protect people or protect yourself

Under pressure, leaders either absorb it and shield their team, or they transmit it downward. Your people know which one you do. They've been watching longer than you realize.

Ask yourself: "When I'm stressed, does my team feel it? Should they?"
3

Whether your values are real or decorative

It's easy to champion your values when there's no cost. The high-pressure moment is when they either hold or they don't. Your team knows the difference between the value you talk about and the one you actually live.

Ask yourself: "What value did I compromise the last time I was under real pressure?"

The Answer
B — Own it, then fix it

Option A might feel like honesty but it's deflection — and your team will know. It reveals a leader who protects their own reputation over their people. Option C avoids accountability. Option D can be appropriate in complex situations but here it's avoidance dressed as caution. Option B is the character move: take ownership, be clear about what's being done, and move forward. It's uncomfortable for about 30 seconds. It builds trust that lasts for months. Pressure just revealed which kind of leader you are — make sure you like what it shows.
