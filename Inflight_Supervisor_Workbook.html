Practice Notes: The 30-Second Check-In
The 3-step structure to follow during practice:
1. Open — Ask a genuine question. Not operational. Not "did you get the brief?"
2. Listen — Actually wait for the answer. Don't fill the pause.

The Seriously Nice Paradox
Avoiding a hard conversation isn't kindness — it's avoidance. What's a conversation you've been telling yourself you're "not ready" for — when really, you've been protecting yourself, not them?
The SBI Framework
S
Situation
Set the specific time and place — anchor the conversation in fact.
B
Behavior
Name what they did — not who they are. Observable action only.
I
Impact
Say what it cost — the team, the guest, the operation, or you.
Key reminder: Behavior, not character. What they did, not who they are.

My 5-Minute Debrief Template
Use this with your FAs after any hard situation — a difficult flight, a conflict, a near-miss, a rough passenger interaction. It takes five minutes. It builds the habit of learning out loud.
How to Open It
Say this to start: "Before we move on, I want to spend about 5 minutes on what just happened. I'm going to ask you four questions — I just want honest answers. There's no blame here. This is about what we learn and take forward."

Then go quiet. Ask each question in order. Keep your reactions neutral — the goal is learning, not judgment. Close with one clear takeaway the whole group agrees on.
