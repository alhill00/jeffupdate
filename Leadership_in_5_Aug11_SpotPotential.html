Spot Potential, Not Just Performance

The person ready for more might not be your top performer today.

Your highest performer and your highest-potential person

might not be the same person.

Most leaders promote based on who’s doing the best work right now. That makes sense — they’ve earned it, and it feels safe. But performance measures what someone does today. Potential measures what they can become. The two overlap, but they aren’t the same.

The leaders who build the deepest benches are the ones who learn to see both.

The Tool

4 Signs You’re Looking at High Potential
 

Sign 1

They absorb feedback and you can watch them change.

Not just accept it — actively apply it. The next week, they’re doing something differently. You didn’t have to say it twice.
 

Sign 2

They own their mistakes — without being asked.

They don’t blame the schedule, the system, or the last crew. They say: “That was on me. Here’s what I’m doing about it.” That kind of accountability is rare — and it scales.
 

Sign 3

They think beyond their own role.

They ask questions about decisions that weren’t theirs. They notice what’s happening on the edges. That systems-level thinking is a leading indicator — it’s how people grow into bigger roles before they have the title.
 

Sign 4

They make the people around them better.

They don’t hoard what they know. They bring teammates up. You can see the ripple — the crew runs smoother when they’re around. That’s leadership already happening, without the title.

Sound Familiar?

You promoted your top performer. Six months later, they’re struggling — not because they aren’t good, but because the skills that made them excellent individually don’t automatically scale to leading others.

That wasn’t a performance problem. It was a selection problem. You promoted output when you needed to develop potential.
 

Quick Pulse — 1 Question

Right now, do you have a clear sense of who on your team has the highest long-term potential — separate from who’s your best performer today?

Your Challenge This Week

Think of one person on your team who shows two or more of these signs — even if their current performance isn’t at the top of the list. Have a development conversation this week. Start with: “I see more in you than your current role shows. Let’s talk about it.”
