Leadership in 5

The 2-Minute Connection

Make every brief interaction count — even when you're moving fast.
Be Seriously Nice | Building Relationships

You pass your team members in the hall. You have 2 minutes before your next meeting. Most leaders default to "Hey, how's it going?" and keep walking. That's a missed opportunity. Here's how to make those 2 minutes matter.

The 4-Part Framework
1

Use their name

It's the most powerful word in any language. Don't skip it.

"Hey Marcus — glad I caught you."
2

Reference something specific

Show you're paying attention. Mention something real — a project, a win, a challenge they mentioned.

"I saw how you handled that gate change yesterday."
3

Ask one real question

Not "how are you?" — something that shows genuine curiosity about them.

"What's one thing I can help clear off your plate this week?"
4

Listen to the answer

Two minutes means you only ask one question. But you actually hear the response. Don't check your phone. Don't look past them.

Skip these

  • "How's it going?" while walking past (you don't want the real answer)
  • Launching straight into a task or request (that's not connection — that's delegation)
  • The double-screen glance (phone in hand, eyes on them, brain somewhere else)
This Week's Challenge

Have 3 two-minute connections this week.
Name + something specific + one real question.
Notice what changes.

📊

Why it works: Research shows that employees who feel "seen" by their leader are 4.6x more likely to feel empowered to do their best work. Two minutes of real attention beats an hour of surface-level meetings.
