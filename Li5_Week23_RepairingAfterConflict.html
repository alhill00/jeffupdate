Repairing After Conflict

Most leaders dread the moment after conflict.

The best ones know it's exactly where trust is built.

Conflict between people isn't the problem — it's what happens next. Left unaddressed, even small friction calcifies into distance, resentment, and a team that quietly stops being honest with each other. The leaders who earn the deepest loyalty aren't the ones who never have conflict. They're the ones who know how to repair it.

The Tool

The 4-Step Repair Script

1

Name It

Acknowledge something happened without exaggerating or minimizing. Try: "I noticed things felt off between us — I want to address it."

2

Own Your Part

Even if you're 20% responsible, own it fully and first. "I came in hot and I could have handled that better." No qualifiers, no buts.

3

Ask Before Explaining

Before you share your side, ask: "Can you help me understand how that landed for you?" Then stop talking. Just listen.

4

Reset Together

Close with a forward statement framed as a question: "I value working well with you — can we reset?" Make it an invitation, not a declaration.
 

Sound Familiar?

Your team had a tense shift handoff last week. Words were exchanged, things kept moving, and nobody said anything. Now there's a quiet awkwardness in the room — people being overly polite, tiptoeing around each other. The repair conversation is the one nobody's having.

Great leaders don't wait for tension to "blow over" — because it rarely does. They step into the discomfort first. That's what makes it safe for others to do the same.

Your Challenge This Week

Identify one relationship on your team that has even a small amount of unresolved friction. It doesn't have to be dramatic — an awkward exchange, a missed handoff, a conversation that ended badly. Use the 4-step repair script within 48 hours. Send a message, stop by their workspace, make the call. The longer you wait, the harder it gets — and the more it costs your team.
