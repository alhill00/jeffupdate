Assume Positive Intent

Most conflict at work isn't born of bad intentions.

It's born of assumed bad intentions.

Before you respond to the email that rubbed you wrong, the decision that felt like a slight, or the peer who "went around you" — pause. The most trusting teams operate from a default assumption: people are trying. Not perfectly, but genuinely. That one assumption changes everything about how you lead and how your team relates to each other.

The Tool

The API Check — Assume Positive Intent

1

Pause Before Reacting

When something lands badly, give yourself 24 hours before responding emotionally. Ask: what's the most generous interpretation of what just happened?

2

Name the Story

Say the narrative you're telling yourself out loud: "The story I'm making up is..." Then check it against actual facts. You'll often find the story has outrun the evidence.

3

Ask, Don't Assume

Before concluding someone had bad intent, ask. "Help me understand what happened from your perspective." You'll be surprised how often the answer reframes everything.

 

Sound Familiar?

Your peer sends an email to leadership referencing a project you're leading — without looping you in first. Your first reaction: they're going around you. But when you ask, it turns out they were responding to an urgent exec request and genuinely assumed you were already in the loop.

The story you told yourself almost created a conflict that didn't exist. Assume positive intent first — then investigate.

Your Challenge This Week

When you catch yourself assuming the worst about a colleague's behavior, run the API Check. Name the story, pause, and ask before you react. Do this once this week and notice how the conversation goes differently. The most trusting leaders aren't naive — they're just disciplined about what they assume first.
 

Quick Pulse — 1 Question

Think of a recent situation where you assumed bad intent. When you found out what actually happened — were you right?
